# Time for Patience — When to Sit Out and Wait

Kristjan covers the **hardest skill in trading**: doing nothing. When the market regime is bad, the right move is to sit in cash and wait. This session covers market regime detection, mean reversion trades, and why fundamentals matter for longer-term holds.

---

## Market Regime Detection (Detailed)

Kristjan uses the **Nasdaq 10-day and 20-day moving averages**:

| Regime | 10-day | 20-day | Relationship | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Momentum market** | Rising | Rising | 10 > 20 | Trade aggressively — 2x–4x account |
| **Sideways/choppy** | Flat | Flat | Mixed | Break even; minimal trading |
| **Downtrend** | Sloping down | Sloping down | 10 < 20 | **Sit out. No breakout trading.** |

> "This is where the big money is made — waiting out the bad times. When the 10-day starts rising higher, the 20-day gets higher, the 10 is above the 20 — this is where you double, triple your account."

> "Staying out of the markets is the hardest thing to do. Simple, but not easy."

### The Cycle

1. **Good period** (3–6 months) → double/triple/quadruple account
2. **Bad period** (3–6 months) → sit in cash, go enjoy life
3. **Sideways** → break even at best
4. **Good period again** → repeat

> "Let the amateurs trade, let them churn their accounts, let them blow up. When the times get good again, that's when you double, triple, quadruple your accounts."

## Mean Reversion (Advanced, Not for Beginners)

Kristjan mentions he sometimes trades **mean reversion longs and shorts** during bad markets:

- **Mean reversion long**: Stock oversold, bounces
- **Mean reversion short**: Stock overbought, fades
- **Not recommended** for anyone who hasn't made their first million

> "I'm just trying to teach you a method where you have the least chance to blow up."

## The ADR Scan Setup (Step-by-Step)

Kristjan walks through setting up the scan in TC2000:

1. **Create ADR condition**: New condition → paste ADR formula → save as "ADR 20 days"
2. **Create scan**: Add conditions:
   - Dollar volume > $10M (minimum; scale by account × 50)
   - ADR > 5% (small accounts should use 6%)
   - Price growth rank > 90% (1-month: `C-MinLow(22)`, 3-month: `C-MinLow(67)`, 6-month: `C-MinLow(126)`)
3. **Run three scans**: 1-month, 3-month, 6-month gainers
4. **After market close**: Review results, find setups, add to watchlist
5. **Tune**: If > 50 results, increase ADR, volume, or rank

> "Everyone should have at least $10M volume cutoff. There's no reason to trade stocks that trade less than a few million dollars worth of stock."

## Lessons Learned

1. **Patience is the hardest skill** — sitting in cash for weeks/months is harder than trading. But it's where the big money is made (waiting for the right regime).
2. **Market regime first** — check Nasdaq 10/20-day before any breakout trading. Bad regime = no trading.
3. **Opinions are worthless** — trade the setup, not your feelings about the company. W went up 42% after someone told Kristjan to quit trading.
4. **Fundamentals = fuel for big moves** — triple-digit earnings/revenue growth drives the biggest multi-month moves. Momentum alone works for short swings.
5. **Blowing up is blowing up** — whether one trade or a thousand small cuts. Protect your account above all.
6. **TC2000 costs $25/month** — invest in the right tools. It's the price of entry for serious trading.
