# Jan 2017 Tracking Watchlists Cleaning — Pt 2 (Jan 12–31)

Kristjan continues his January review, showcasing how **shakeouts after breakout** and **earnings catalysts** drive follow-through — and how his biggest regret (AAOI) came from a tight setup he recognized perfectly but still didn't buy.

---

## Theme: Shakeouts and Earnings Cappers

The second half of January is dominated by:
- **Earnings breakouts** — the cleanest catalyst for follow-through
- **Shakeout candles** — the day after a breakout that takes out the prior day's lows, then reverses higher
- **Rising moving average bounces** — 20/50/100/150-day support holding, then breaking out again

> "These kinds can really accelerate... it looks like they're overextended but then they accelerate and make an insane move. If you can just find a spot where you can put in a tight stop these things can give a really good risk-to-reward trade."

## Bankrupt / Fraud Names — A Recurring Category

Kristjan explicitly tracks and sometimes trades bankrupt/fraud stocks because **momentum overrides fundamentals**:

> "It's a fraud, China reverse merger, but I guess they're pumping it or something... went up 82%, 90% almost."

> "Bankrupt stocks can run 500% even though everyone knows they're bankrupt... they run the shorts, everyone thinks it's an easy short, then momentum comes in and they go up a lot."

He only skipped one because **commissions + ECN fees** on low-priced names ate the edge — not because of the fundamentals.

## The "Shakeout Then Straight Up" Pattern

Several stocks stopped him out at/near breakeven and then went straight up for weeks:

> "I bought it on the opening range highs at $660, got stopped out the opening range lows at $635, it went all the way down to $6.15 and then it just reversed later in the day, took out the highs, went like $7 and had a couple days of follow through... these kinds of things happen all the time."

**Lesson:** Stops near intraday lows of gap-up days are vulnerable to shakeouts. He doesn't change the rule, but acknowledges the cost.

## Mid-Range vs. High/Low Setups

> "I usually like stocks that are near their 52-week highs or six-month highs or that are near the lows, but this was kind of a mid-range... What it did have going for it is that had some intermediate term momentum, no short term momentum but it was in a tight range."

He mostly skips mid-range bases — prefers setups at the extremes of the range.

## Notable Quotes

> "Sometimes it's just better to buy the one that looks more extended because there's a reason that it is more extended usually."

> "It's easier with earnings in my opinion." (vs. political/government catalysts)

> "These kinds can really accelerate... it looks like they're overextended but then they accelerate and make an insane move."

## Lessons

1. **AAOI is the canonical "I saw it, I knew it, I didn't buy it" trade.** The biggest regret of the year — the setup was textbook and the catalyst (earnings) was clean. Pattern recognition without execution is worthless.
2. **Earnings = cleanest catalyst.** Political/government decisions are harder to have conviction on.
3. **Shakeouts are a tax on tight stops.** Accept the cost or the stops don't protect you.
4. **Extended stocks can keep extending.** Don't auto-pass on "too extended" — there's usually a fundamental reason for the extension.
5. **Bankrupt/fraud names can run 500%.** Short interest + momentum > fundamentals in the short term.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **The biggest regrets aren't bad trades — they're the textbook setups you didn't take.**
2. **Earnings catalysts** produce the cleanest, most tradeable follow-through.
3. **Shakeout candles** (taking out prior day's lows then reversing) are a recurring cost of tight-stop discipline.
4. **Bankrupt/short-interest names** can produce enormous moves despite (because of) the fundamental hate.
5. **Mid-range bases** are a low-priority setup — stick to range extremes.
