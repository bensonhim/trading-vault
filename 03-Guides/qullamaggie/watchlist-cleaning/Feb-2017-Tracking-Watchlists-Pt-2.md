# Feb 2017 Tracking Watchlists Cleaning — Pt 2 (Feb 10–28)

The back half of February is a graveyard of **one-day wonders** — Kristjan's repeated label for breakouts that pop on day one and die. The few multi-day movers are earnings-driven, and the recurring lesson is patience: he stopped out of earnings names too early when they undercut rising moving averages.

---

## One-Day Wonders — The Dominant Pattern

Kristjan uses the phrase "one day wonder" repeatedly. Most breakouts in mid-late Feb fail on day two. The drivers:
- **Analyst upgrades** (most fail — "most gap ups on analyst upgrades just fail")
- **S&P 500 additions** (one-day wonders, he says explicitly)
- **Random PR / promotions** (chat-room pumps, Twitter pumps)
- **Mediocre earnings** (slight beat, no growth)

The setups that work are the ones with **real catalysts**: big earnings beats, guidance raises, FDA/drug trial news, sector themes.

## The Earnings Stop-Out Mistake (Key Lesson)

> "This was one of my biggest mistakes I made so far this year: getting stopped out of these earnings trades when they undercut one of these rising moving averages. I think all of the cases were when it undercut the rising twenty day and then they just went back into the range and had more follow through. So this is something I gotta be more patient with earnings related names."

**Correction:** For earnings-capper names, give the stock more room after the breakout — the undercut of the rising 20-day is often a shakeout, not a failure. Tighter stops work on breakouts; looser stops on earnings follow-through.

## OLED — The Micro-Cap Pump Warning

> "These micro cap names many times they get volume because all these chat rooms are pumping it and then they just die the next day."

OLED had a **five-star chart** — higher lows, flag, range break, volume — and it still failed because the volume was chat-room-driven, not institutional. **Volume source matters, not just volume.**

## The Kramer Effect (Luck Component)

> "If I had known that Kramer was gonna feature this one... I had a perfect breakout then I had some insane follow through the next day but I wasn't aware that that was gonna happen."

A stock he was long had a perfect breakout; the next day's gap was driven by a Kramer (CNBC) feature he didn't anticipate. He sold into the pop for +50%. **Media features are a tailwind you can't predict but can ride.**

## IPO Dave — Secondary Offering Edge

> "Everyone should follow this guy IPO Dave. He's very good with the IPOs and secondary price offerings... He buys a lot of these secondaries with a tight stop and a lot of times stocks go up on secondary offerings if it's done for the right reason like to expand the business."

**Secondary offering trade:** When a company prices a secondary for business expansion (not survival), funds use it as an entry mechanism — the stock often rallies post-pricing. Buy the secondary with a tight stop.

## Extended Stocks Keep Extending

> "This one I almost bought it, I think I bought it here and here but I missed this one but only had one day of follow through. Again I usually look for at least three to five days of follow through."

He requires **3–5 days of follow-through** as a baseline. One day is noise.

## Lessons

1. **One-day wonders are the default failure mode.** Most breakouts fail on day two — analyst upgrades, S&P additions, PR, mediocre earnings all produce them.
2. **Volume source matters.** Chat-room-driven volume on micro-caps = pump; institutional volume on real catalysts = follow-through.
3. **Earnings cappers need looser stops.** Undercut of rising 20-day is often a shakeout, not a failure — his biggest self-identified mistake of the period.
4. **Secondary offerings for expansion** can be bought with tight stops — funds use them as entry points.
5. **3–5 days of follow-through** is the minimum to validate a breakout.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **One-day wonders dominate.** Most breakouts die on day two — filter for real catalysts (earnings, guidance, FDA) over noise (upgrades, PR, S&P adds).
2. **Patience on earnings names:** the rising-20-day undercut is a shakeout, not a stop signal.
3. **Volume source > volume size** — chat-room pumps produce volume that dies; institutional volume persists.
4. **Secondary offerings** (for expansion, not survival) are a buyable setup with tight stops.
5. **Media features** (Kramer, Barron's) are unpredictable tailwinds — ride them, don't anticipate them.
