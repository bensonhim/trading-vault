# March 2017 Tracking Watchlists Cleaning — Pt 1 (Mar 1–14)

Early March 2017 was a **failed-breakout festival** — clean setups at rising moving averages failed repeatedly, and the wins came from low-priced micro-cap momentum runs and one textbook trend ride that Kristjan calls his best trade of the month.

---

## The Failed-Breakout Theme

March 1–3 is remarkable for how many **good-looking setups at rising moving averages failed**:
- [[FCX]] — undercut rising 100-day, looked like a bounce, "complete failure"
- [[CA]] — bounced off rising 150-day, "looked like a good setup but just couldn't master a bounce"
- Multiple breakouts on strong commodity days → no follow-through

> "Some days you get a lot of great looking breakouts but you can never be sure which ones are gonna have follow through and which ones are not gonna follow through. So you kind of have to pick the best ones that fit your criteria and just hope for the best."

## The Best Trade of the Month (Unnamed, ~$1.40 entry)

> "This was one of my better trades, one of my bigger trades during the month of March, bought it there, just a perfect setup. First leg up sideways, higher lows all the way. Look at how it rode this twenty day rising moving average higher, just higher lows going into the breakout... I bought it in the 1.40s, I think below $1.50... Next day, gap, this was on just pure momentum. There were no news or anything. Just the sector is very hot."

**Anatomy:**
1. First leg up (initial momentum)
2. Sideways consolidation
3. Higher lows riding the **rising 20-day**
4. Breakout on momentum (no news — sector-driven)
5. Gap up next day

He sold most shares too early and missed the continued run up (and thankfully the run down). **Textbook trend ride.**

## The MOMO Screw-Up

> "I was long like 6,000 shares and I was up over $20 [thousand] and then I got stopped out for a small loss and then it's straight up since and just very annoying."

Classic case of a winning trade turned into a small loss by a stop, then the stock went straight up. The frustration is audible. This is the recurring **earnings-capper stop-out problem** from the Feb Pt 2 review — he's still working on it.

## The "Almost Bought" Regret Pattern

[[VREH]] (V-Ray):
> "Should have bought it but I didn't... I thought seven was a realistic target. Not bad, so really five star setup at volume and everything and tight range candles before, multiple tight range candles before the breakout."

> "Unfortunately those ones are the worst. You go look back at something you almost bought. You literally sat on a trigger and then you didn't buy it and then you look back on it and it's up straight up since. That always sucks."

## Micro-Cap Momentum Runs

March 7–10 had a cluster of low-priced micro-cap runs:
- [[INNL]] — bought, next day went nuts, sold all on fluff news, one more leg higher then crapped out
- Several other micro-caps ran on no news, just sector/momentum

> "All the micro cap names, these bottoming the low price bottom micro caps were running."

These are tradeable but sizing is capped — he took the trades but with smaller size.

## Parabolic Short Watch

> "This one I'm actually gonna put into my short watch list. It's starting to go parabolic here or a little bit at least. The short will probably be tomorrow."

He explicitly flags parabolic extensions for shorts — this is the setup hierarchy from the [[Trading Meeting Knowledge|knowledge base]] (parabolic short / WSS).

## Lessons

1. **Moving-average bounces fail too.** A clean setup at a rising 50/100/150-day is not a guarantee — March 1 had a cluster of failures.
2. **The best trade of the month had no news** — it was sector momentum + textbook trend ride on the rising 20-day.
3. **Earnings-capper stop-outs are still a problem.** MOMO is the canonical example — $20k winner → small loss → straight up.
4. **The "almost bought" regret is the worst kind.** Sitting on the trigger and not pulling it.
5. **Micro-cap runs are tradeable but size-capped.** Take them, but don't expect to make the whole month on them.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Good-looking setups fail** — March 1 was a cluster of moving-average bounce failures. You can never be sure which follows through.
2. **The best trade of the month** = first leg up → sideways on rising 20-day → higher lows → breakout on sector momentum (no news needed).
3. **Earnings-capper stop discipline** is still the work-in-progress — winners turned to small losses are the biggest leak.
4. **Micro-cap momentum runs** cluster — trade them with small size, expect one-day wonders mixed in.
5. **Parabolic extensions** go on the short watch list — they're a setup, not just an observation.
