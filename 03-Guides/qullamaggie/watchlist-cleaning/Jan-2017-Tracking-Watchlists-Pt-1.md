# Jan 2017 Tracking Watchlists Cleaning — Pt 1 (Jan 3–9)

Kristjan walks through his daily tracking watchlists for early January 2017, judging each stock by **follow-through** — the single filter that separates real moves from one-day wonders — and filing the good-looking charts into a multi-year database for future pattern recall.

---

## The Tracking Watchlist Method

Every day Kristjan drops stocks of interest (breakouts, breakdowns, news gaps, earnings cappers) into a watchlist named by date. Weeks/months later he reviews each one to see whether it had **follow-through** — what conditions moved it, what catalyst was needed, and what magnitude. This is a pattern-recognition drill, not a trade log.

> "I have built an enormous chart database with thousands and thousands of stocks over the past ten, fifteen, twenty years... it's a good exercise just going back looking at charts and looking at stocks with news and the same kind of patterns, they occur over and over again."

He warns this is a "long and boring" exercise done for self-education, not entertainment.

## Setup & Entry Mechanics

**Preferred breakout anatomy** (the "textbook" he keeps citing):
1. Initial leg up → establishes short-term momentum
2. Sideways rest, preferably on the 20-day moving average
3. Tight range candle(s) before breakout
4. Breakout **on volume**
5. Multi-day follow-through (he targets 3–5 days, ideally weeks)

**Entry method:**
> "I buy them on the opening range highs and that's usually like the first thirty minute highs but sometimes I do them earlier maybe the first five or ten or fifteen minute highs, it depends on the volume and a lot of factors."

**Stop:** candle lows (often prior day's low or the breakout candle's low).

## Why He Passes on Setups (Recurring Reasons)

| Reason | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Too thin / low-priced** | SALT, ORX, SPLK — commissions + ECN fees kill low-price names even when the move is huge |
| **Already extended** | Stocks up 2+ days in a row feel like chasing |
| **Earnings/data imminent** | Biotechs with scheduled data — too risky to hold |
| **Already in a lot of stuff** | Position risk caps how many trades he takes |
| **Bad-looking setup** | Wide range candle day before, no tightness |
| **News = upgrade only** | "Upgrades tend to not have great follow through" — he passed on a fast-growing stock purely because the catalyst was an upgrade, not news he respected |

> "If there had been no news on it I would have been buying it. That's how my mind works."

## Lessons

1. **Follow-through is the judge.** A setup is only validated by what happens in the 3–5 days after the breakout candle.
2. **Catalog the good ones.** Every chart with great follow-through goes into his multi-decade chart database — an investment in pattern memory that compounds.
3. **Thin stocks can move the most but you can't size them.** A $10k position in a thin 70%-mover is still capped; he prefers liquid names where he can add size.
4. **Sometimes pass for catalyst reasons, not chart reasons.** Upgrades, gap-downs on bad earnings, and scheduled biotech data are structural avoid-lists for him.
5. **Bad setups sometimes work better than good setups.** He still tracks sloppy breakouts to learn what follows through.
6. **Pass rate is high.** He passes on the vast majority of what he sees — selectivity is the edge.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. The tracking watchlist is a **feedback loop**: log → wait → review follow-through → internalize which conditions produce moves.
2. **Tight range + volume + breakout** is the recurring anatomy; wide candles the day before are a red flag.
3. **Opening range highs** (first 30 min, sometimes 5–15 min) is his standard long entry trigger.
4. **Thin/low-priced stocks** are real edge killers — the move can be enormous but position size is capped by liquidity and fees.
5. Longevity in trading comes from **building a chart database over years** — the same patterns recur.
