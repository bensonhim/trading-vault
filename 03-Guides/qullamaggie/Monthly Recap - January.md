# Monthly Recap — January ($71K Profit, 41% Win Rate)

Kristjan made **$71K** in January with a **41% win rate** — but the month was defined by three categories of mistakes: anticipating breakdowns on extended stocks, failing to size in on microcap range breaks, and being too patient on real-stock shorts.

## Mistake Category 1: Anticipating Breakdowns on Extended Stocks

Kristjan tried to short overextended stocks before they actually broke down — took multiple small losses (~$1K each) before finally nailing the trade, usually just making back prior losses.

| Stock | Action | Result | Lesson |
|-------|--------|--------|--------|
| [[GS]] | Shorted before trendline break | 4–5 small losses, finally nailed it | Wait for trendline break confirmation |
| [[JPM]] | Same — shorted before breakdown | Small losses | No reason to short before confirmed break |
| [[AMD]] | Anticipated breakdown below 20-day | Wasted money over 3–4 trades | Wait for confirmation |
| [[REN]] | Expected pullback that never came | Only trade he didn't make money back on | Don't fight momentum without confirmation |

> "There was really no reason to short it before the stock actually broke this trend line, but I tried to anticipate it... just waste of time and effort and mental energy."

> "I just anticipated the most extended stocks to have a little bit of pullbacks and that was my mistake, I just should have waited for confirmation."

## Mistake Category 2: Too-Tight Stops on Big Movers

### [[BIOC]] — The Painful One

- Enormous momentum run, came off lows, traded many times its float
- 60-min chart: higher lows all day, late-day range breakout
- **Entry**: 50,000 shares in mid-$1.60s
- Stock popped then came back into range
- **Mistake**: Stopped out at breakeven with ~10¢ stop instead of keeping the higher low as stop
- Stock gapped up enormously next day, went up another $1.60–$1.70
- **Loss**: ~$500 (stopped for a penny)
- **Missed profit**: Could have been a very big trade with very small risk

> "Sometimes too tight stops lead you to miss out the real move and this was one of those times."

## Mistake Category 5: Rule-Breaking on [[MYOS]]

The one trade where Kristjan broke multiple rules:

1. ❌ **Shorted on day one** of a runner (usually never does this)
2. ❌ **Randomly shorted into strength and added into strength** (only does this on day 3/4 near open)
3. ❌ **Didn't cut size when intraday ranges broke to the upside**
4. ❌ **No set stop**

- Started shorting in high $3s, stock was up ~200%
- Did tiny size each time but accumulated 7,000 shares
- Down ~$15–16K at highs
- Covered on pullback before close
- **Final loss**: $8,000

> "Thank God I did tiny size because I kind of unconsciously knew that I shouldn't be doing this shit."

> "I used to do this kind of thing a lot in my earlier years... sometimes I ended up taking multiple 6 figure losses on these things over the years and it's just so stupid."

## Mistake Category 6: Swing Longs Without Sector Context

### [[UEC]] and [[URG]] (Uranium Stocks)

- Bought both on breakout patterns — looked good and tight on 60-min chart, low risk
- **Mistake**: Didn't check the [[URA]] (Uranium ETF) chart — it was very extended
- UEC and URG were lagging the main uranium stocks
- "This kind of a short is very vulnerable pullback"
- Stopped out for small losses
- Saw URA chart next day, closed positions quickly

## Key Takeaways

1. **Never anticipate breakdowns** — wait for confirmation (trendline breaks, range breaks).
2. **Don't use too-tight stops on big movers** — keep the higher low as your stop.
3. **Size in on microcap range breaks** — when a runner finally cracks its intraday range, that's the high-probability add spot. Day 3–4 are best.
4. **Take profits on real-stock shorts** — institutional support means they bounce; don't expect 30–50% drops like microcaps.
5. **Never break multiple rules at once** — if you do, at least use tiny size.
6. **Always check the sector ETF** before buying lagging stocks in an extended sector.
7. **Every month, eliminating the top 5 mistakes could add 50–100% more profit.**
