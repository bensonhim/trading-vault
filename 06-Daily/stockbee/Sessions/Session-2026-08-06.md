# SNDK Earnings Test, Sunken Cost Fallacy, and Cleopatra the Original Sugar Baby

August 6 was the day SNDK earnings would test the market narrative. Pradeep framed it: every market turn has a narrative change. The current narrative was "shorts covering because Leopold blew up and Citadel bought." Now the question was whether SNDK, WDC, STX earnings would change the narrative again. The AM session watched SNDK hold up while WDC and STX "took it on the chin." Datadog and CrowdStrike insider selling (20+ insiders) was flagged as a red flag. A member down 74% on SNDK following a guru who had already sold everything — the sunken cost fallacy in real time. The PM session found more bearish setups than bullish, Palantir as the only delayed reaction DEP candidate, and Newell Rubbermaid as the only anticipation. The session featured the origin of "sugar baby" — Julius Caesar and Cleopatra, the original sugar baby at age 21.

### Sunken Cost Fallacy — Member Down 74% on SNDK

> "A member logged into SNDK based on some guru he followed. Down 74%. Had high hope of earnings. I told him: whatever is left, take it and run. I might have to attend a funeral."

> "The worst part: the guru he was following tweeted two weeks ago that he sold everything and is in cash. All these followers yoloed in because that time he was saying he was yoloing. And he already exited."

> "Sunken cost is the biggest fallacy in life. History is full of empires that blew up because of sunken cost. It paralyzes people from making decisions."

> "There is no yoloing in this business. Don't listen to anybody — develop a setup and do your own thinking."

### Julius Caesar and Cleopatra — Original Sugar Baby

> "Julius Caesar was 52 when he met 21-year-old Cleopatra. She was the first original sugar baby. She was ruthless, very thinking, had all her cards in place."

> "After Caesar was killed, she hooked up with Mark Antony. She knew her game."

> "Somebody seriously asked me in a Zoom meeting: why did you name Sugar Babies 'Sugar Babies'? I told him about Julius Caesar. He said: you're a crazy guy."
