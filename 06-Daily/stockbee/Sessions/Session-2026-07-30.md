# OODA Loop, Cockroach Theory, and the Leopold Blow-Up — Situational Awareness Saves Your Plane

July 30 was a teaching masterpiece built around a real-time market event: the forced liquidation of a major fund (Leopold's "Situational Awareness" fund — $100B+ portfolio, Citadel bought $10B of it overnight). Pradeep used the event to teach the OODA loop (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act, developed by the US Air Force for pilot situational awareness), the cockroach theory ("when you find one, there are more"), and cognitive dissonance as the psychological trap that killed the fund. The market bounced (Day 4-5 of decline = mean reversion), GLW reversal bullish worked (up 8%), Microsoft earnings provided a good SIPs/DEP candidate, and CLSK showed signs of life with its data center catalyst. Pradeep remained cautiously bullish — not "kiss your cousin" bullish, but bullish. The session ended early (dentist appointment).

## AM Meeting (9:15 AM)

### SA: OODA Loop — The Framework for Situational Awareness

Pradeep opened with a deep dive into the origin of situational awareness:

> "Situational awareness is a methodology developed by the US Air Force during the Vietnam War. They were losing 95% of planes because pilots lost situational awareness."

> "The solution was OODA: Observe the environment. Orient yourself to understand what's happening. Decide a course of action. Act. Then observe again. It's a continuous loop."

> "Same in trading: if your trading is not working, you change your setup. You don't blame Biden, Israel, Palestine, Iran, Trump, or policy. You correct your course, or your plane crashes."

### All Quantitative Systems Have Regime Filters

> "Even quantitative funds have a regime filter. Momentum works in a particular regime. Value works in a certain regime. All quant systems have regime filters."

> "Retail traders who start backtesting without regime filters get 50% drawdowns. When they add the filter, performance improves."

> "You have to operate with situational awareness if you're doing swing trading, position trading, or long-term investing. If you don't have that skill, you're always going to be the person who cries."

### Breakouts Selectively, Don't Short on Day 3-4

> "Breakouts likely to work? Not an absolute no. Some will work if they have a clear catalyst or EP 9M."

> "Shorting the market when it's down 3-4 days in a row is the worst thing you can do. You're late to the party. Friday was the day."

> "Market down 4 days in a row → probability of a short-term bounce is very high. But beyond today, you can't decide whether this continues."

### Microsoft — SIPs Today, DEP Candidate

> "Microsoft has good earnings today. It's a SIPs. But typically, Microsoft is better bought as a DEP. It doesn't go like a bullet — glacial move. It might give you a better entry with lower risk."

> "I'm definitely bullish on Microsoft, but finding a low-risk entry is the question."

### NTRT/MTRT = 90% of EP 9M Already Known

> "If you do NTRT/MTRT, 90% of EP 9M you would know even before they hit your scan. Microsoft — you already knew it was going up today."

> "It's a process. You have to marry the process. But to marry it, you need conviction and confidence."

### Combination Bullish Finds Everything

> "Everything which is going to make a 20% move, 100% of the time, will show up in combination scan on the first day of the move. It's just a question of whether on that day it met your criteria."

> "When it goes up 3-4 days, you feel you should have bought it. But on Day 1, would you have qualified it?"

### Anticipation — Morbid Fascination

> "People are tremendously fascinated by anticipation, which has the lowest win rate. And they dismiss EP 9M as 'too late.' But after EP 9M, it can make a move of 48%."

> "There is a morbid fascination with anticipation, but it's the least important setup."

### Ask Yourself: "Is There a Setup?"

> "Before taking a trade, ask yourself out loud: 'Is there a setup? What is the setup?' It has stopped me from taking shit setups."

### Leadership Books Recommendation

> "John Kotter — 'What Leaders Really Do.' The key role of leadership is to raise people's aspiration level. If you raise it, they find solutions. If you can't, they sit and cry."

> "Warren Bennis — 'Leaders.' These are the two foundational books."

### Margin Call — The Movie

> "I highly recommend watching Margin Call. It's all about this. If you have a leveraged position, you're always going to get into this kind of trouble."

> "The key scene: the morning meeting. 'There are three ways to make money in the market: be first, be smart, or cheat. Being first is easiest.' And he deleverages the entire portfolio by 2:00."

### Cognitive Dissonance Killed the Fund

> "Why didn't he sell into strength? Because of belief. Belief drives behavior. His belief was that he was right and everybody else was wrong."

> "Cognitive dissonance: if your belief is that AI stocks will go up 10x, and the market says otherwise, you get paralyzed."

## Key Takeaways

1. **OODA Loop** — Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. The Air Force's situational awareness framework applies directly to trading. If your trading isn't working, change course — don't blame external factors.
2. **All quant systems have regime filters** — Momentum works in a regime. Value works in a regime. Without a regime filter, 50% drawdowns are inevitable.
3. **Leopold blow-up = cockroach theory** — When one fund blows up, there are more. Forced liquidation creates volatility but doesn't mean V-shaped recovery.
4. **Cognitive dissonance kills** — Belief that "I'm right, everyone else is wrong" → paralysis → no selling into strength → blow-up. The fund named "Situational Awareness" lost situational awareness.
5. **Three L's: Leverage, Liquor, Ladies** — Jesse Livermore lost his money on sugar babies, not trading. Leverage amplifies all mistakes.
6. **Margin Call the movie** — "Be first, be smart, or cheat." The fund manager deleveraged by 2:00 PM. Same movie repeats throughout history (1907, 1932, 1976, 1942).
7. **Don't short on Day 3-4** — Market down 4 days → bounce probability very high. Friday was the day to short.
8. **Microsoft = SIPs today, DEP candidate** — Good earnings, glacial move, better entry with lower risk as DEP. Might go to $500.
9. **GLW = A-class reversal bullish** — Perfect exhaustion setup. Entered day before yesterday, worked today. Textbook example.
10. **NTRT/MTRT = 90% of EP 9M known** — If you do your morning process, most EP 9M candidates are already known before the scan runs.
11. **Combination scan finds everything** — 100% of stocks that make 20%+ moves show up in combination scan on Day 1. Question is whether you qualify them.
12. **Anticipation has lowest win rate** — "Morbid fascination" with the worst setup. People dismiss EP 9M as "too late" but it can make 48% after the signal.
13. **"Is there a setup?" — ask out loud** — Self-check before every trade. Stops you from taking shit setups.
14. **Cautiously bullish** — Not kiss-your-cousin bullish, but bullish. Another month of chop near lows might be best for a big move. Watch EP 9M for 15 days for fund allocation signals.
15. **Kiss on the first date** — If you don't try, you'll never know. OPG and pray.
