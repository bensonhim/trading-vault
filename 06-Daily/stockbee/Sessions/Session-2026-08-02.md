# Reflex Bounce After Leopold — Information Diet, Bobo Dolls, and the Best Contraceptive

August 2 was the first session after the Leopold fund blow-up became public knowledge. The market had a reflex bounce (Day 4-5 of decline = mean reversion), but Pradeep was not impressed — gaps everywhere, everything fading by PM, no tight setups, no bottom bounce structure on SPY. The AM session was a deep dive into the Citadel/Goldman deal mechanics, how Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan were already positioning for this in the first week of June (same time as the London Calling video), and how anyone with situational awareness could have seen the signals. The PM session introduced Albert Bandura's Bobo Doll experiment as a metaphor for information diet — you become what you consume. Software stocks that rallied on the forced liquidation squeeze may be short candidates as they correct. GLW was the textbook exhaustion trade that everyone doubted but worked perfectly. Pradeep is cautiously bullish long-term but expects "London weather" (choppy) through September-October.

---

## AM Meeting (9:15 AM)

### SA: Reflex Bounce, Not Impressed

> "Wednesday was a better setup than Thursday. On Tuesday, we had a lot of reversal bullish — washout selling. Then the Leopold liquidation news broke and ruined those setups."

> "Yesterday we had a bounce, but the breadth was nothing to write home about. No good bottom bounce structure on SPY. Difficult to hang your hat with conviction saying this is the end."

### Two Scenarios

> "Scenario 1: Small bounce, revisit lows, after a few weeks of back and forth, the market resolves to the upside. Scenario 2: V-shaped bounce develops. Possible, but what's the probability?"

> "I always believed this correction will resolve to the upside. This is a choppy period which will ultimately resolve. But in the short run, lot of setups yesterday are just dead-in-the-water stocks that bounced."

### Oracle = Only Institutional Quality Bottom Bounce

> "Oracle is the one I see as an institutional quality bottom bounce. But there's no tightness. I'd rather buy something once the rally starts going than put money and probably get an edge of doubt."

### What Works: EP 9M, SIPs, DEP

> "Default setups: EP 9M, DEP, SIPs. One day wonders. I would not look at anything major. But there are some better looking setups which might take another two or three days."

> "I went through EP 9M for the last one month. I looked at momentum stocks by 25 days, 65 days, top 100. I don't see a lot of stocks lining up with good constructive patterns currently."

### Software Stocks = Short Candidates

> "Some of the moves in software stocks, which happened because of the squeeze, might start correcting. That might be the short opportunity there. They have more constructive short patterns because it was just an event in the last three or four days."

### BABA = DEP But Gapping Too Much

> "BABA was the DEP, but BABA is also gapping up. If it gaps up, there is no entry. I'm not going to chase that kind of idea."

### Don't Chase — The Best Contraceptive

> "The best contraceptive is not before or after — it's to drink a glass of cold water instead of having sex. That's from a real UN conference."

> "Gaps happen because somebody has some news which you don't have. Price shocks. In order to trade gaps, one should be totally focused on NTRT, MTRT, and doing the morning work."

### Citadel Deal Mechanics

> "Goldman Sachs didn't approach Citadel initially. They approached Jane Street, Millennium, everybody. Then our man Citadel came to know and called because he could see a deal. He could see a bargain."

> "Goldman knew, Citadel knew. These are the two players whom retail traders piss on all the time, but they are the smartest rats on the ship."

> "JPMorgan, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs — they were all telling you in the first week of June. JPMorgan newsletter said 'positioning on the street is too one-sided, we expect a period of chop.' They handle money for people. What do you think they did to their portfolios?"

### Earnings Rejection Was the Signal

> "When these stocks had great earnings and didn't react positively — wasn't that information? You don't need to be a Citadel founder to see that."

> "19% move in two months. If you know anything about market history, that's an abnormal move. Suddenly, the most beautiful women are saying hi to you — wouldn't you look in the mirror?"

### Don't Worry About Japanese Banks

> "When you have $100K to trade, don't worry about Japanese banks or Norwegian sovereign funds. Just worry about: can you find something in this scan, and can you make money from that?"

> "Macro is for people who have billions. Even they don't use it. Just use things which are simple. Find a setup, believe in it, beat the shit out of it every day."

### Sunrise Raider — Dumpster Diving

> "Sunrise Raider's methodology is different. He calls it dumpster diving — buying stocks that have been beaten down. I'm 100% sure he bought GLW. He buys setups like this which others doubt."

> "His process is more evolved than mine — 30/40/30 split instead of 80/20. Read his post. It has more value than looking at Japanese banks."

### Observation = The Key Skill

> "If you want to become good, write down observations. There is learning in every single stock, every period of the market."

> "When you see a move like this on a cyclical stock, and it's at cyclical peak in earnings, and the earnings is so good and the market doesn't react — that's information."

> "Become an Indian — not the Patel kind. The native Indian who had the skill of minute observation. Putting their ear to the ground and sensing: oh, there are four white people coming, one of them is old, maybe there's a child."

### No Overnight Positions

> "Did I buy anything from yesterday? No. I'm not carrying overnight anything. Microsoft was the only thing I had intraday."

### Albert Bandura — Bobo Doll Experiment

> "Albert Bandura's social learning theory: three groups of kids. One group — no role model. Second group — adult beating a Bobo doll. Third group — adult ignoring the Bobo doll."

> "The kids who saw the adult beat the Bobo doll also beat the shit out of it. The kids who saw the adult ignore it just ignored it. We learn socially. We learn from role models."

> "If you're consuming random YouTube videos about macro and random shit, what do you think you're going to be thinking? You are influenced by your environment. Be very clear about controlling your information diet."

> "If your consumption is random, you're going to become random. If you're hanging around with crybabies, you'll become a crybaby."

### Position Sizing

> "I would take three or four positions. You don't need more than three to be fully invested."

> "One wife, one office wife, and one sugar baby. More than enough. Or one husband, one office husband, and one toy boy."

> "If I'm going to invest in something, it should be worthwhile. 100 shares doesn't move your account. Then you have 20 positions and can't manage them."

### 67% Drawdown = Leopold's Lesson

> "They were up 400% to June. Then lost 67%. He's still 80% up for the year, but he was carrying 1:4 leverage. That's probably $100B in leverage."

> "If your account goes down 6-7-8-10%, you should immediately stop trading, sell everything, and reconsider. 67% is game over."

> "After 67% loss, even if you come back to the same level, you're not going to get paid. You're working free. It will take years."

### Members Who Put Everything in One Stock

> "I personally know a member who put all his account — $2-3M — on SNDK, just before June. After doing all the analysis and listening to people, he said 'this is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity.' This is not YOLO. This is suicide."

> "Another member is completely stuck on SpaceX IPO. Every meeting, first 10 minutes I explain IPOs go down. Next hour he explains how Elon Musk is the next god and I don't understand. You're losing money every day. Stop worshiping and learn a setup."

> "I want to become the greatest fisherman in the world, and I don't have a fishing pole. I don't know where the ocean is. I don't have a boat. How can I help these people?"

### GLW — The Textbook Exhaustion

> "GLW was a proper exhaustion. Selling had exhausted. All sellers who had to sell had exhausted. There was nothing."

> "So many people were doubting that setup. That's the setup you should put in your book of reversal bullish. Only small setup reversal bullish are going to work like this. In a year, if you can find 10 or 20 of them, it's worth trading."

> "This is not even exhaustion. It's like asking: does Shakira look like Adele?"

### London Weather Through September-October

> "I think we'll continue in London weather for probably September, late October probably. And then you'll have Caribbean weather for the rest of the year."

### Recovery After Blow-Up

> "I've seen people recover from blow-ups. Sometimes it forces them to confront their issues and make the changes they need to make."

> "After three meetings with one member, I asked: what setups are you trading? He says: I don't have a setup. I don't have software. I don't scan. How can I help you? Tell me."

### DEP Watch List Video This Weekend
